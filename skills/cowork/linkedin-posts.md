# LinkedIn Posts

Write LinkedIn posts in Mirek's voice. The voice is opinionated, plainspoken, and structured around a clear point of view — not a summary, not a recommendation, not a tour of someone else's ideas.

## Core principles

**Lead with the take, not the source.** The post is Mirek's argument; the source is evidence for it. A good opener is a claim, a tension, or a sharp observation — not "I just read X and wanted to share."

**One idea per post.** Pick the one that hit hardest. If two ideas are fighting for the spot, cut one.

**Show position, not appreciation.** Replace "I loved his point about X" with the actual point. Concrete over abstract.

**Earn the closer.** The last line should be quotable on its own — a callback, a reframe, something slightly aphoristic. If it's just a summary, it's wasted.

## Voice notes

- Conversational register. Contractions. Reads like Mirek talking, not writing a memo.
- Mix short sentences with longer ones. Short ones carry weight.
- No corporate-LinkedIn tics: no 🚀, no "Here are 3 lessons 👇", no "What do you think?" closers.
- Title the source in quotes the first time it's named.
- Profanity allowed only when it's already in the source and earns its place.

## Workflow

1. If a URL is provided, fetch it first. Don't write from a vague impression.
2. Find Mirek's angle — what thread does he want to pull? Ask if unclear.
3. Draft one confident version. Not three options — one.
4. After drafting: identify the weakest line, the softest framing, the place it goes descriptive instead of argumentative.
5. Iterate until it's shorter and sharper, not longer.

## Structure (adapt as needed)

1. **Hook** — a claim or tension. One line. Makes the reader want the next one.
2. **Anchor** — one sentence naming the source. The only summary beat allowed.
3. **Argument** — two or three short paragraphs developing the one idea.
4. **Closer** — a line that lands.

## What to avoid

- Reaction-stack openings: "What I liked most about…", "Three things stood out…"
- Meta-commentary: "captures something I resonate with", "really makes you think"
- Generic closers: questions to the audience, calls for engagement, "agree?"
- Long quotes from the source. Paraphrase. Short reference quotes are fine.
- Hedging: "I think maybe", "it could be argued", "in some sense"
